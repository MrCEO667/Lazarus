◆ INTERNAL · PITCHER'S NOTES · NOT FOR THE CEO'S EYES

How to run this meeting.

Ten-minute pitch, twenty-minute discussion. Your job is to make the CEO feel that the expensive thing is not approving Phase 1.

◆ FRAME BEFORE SLIDE 1

"I'm not here to pitch a website. I'm here to show you money we are currently sending to our competitors. This whole deck is built around numbers — our sales team's own words, the trade data, and the unit economics. If any number is wrong, tell me and we'll re-run it in the room on slide 8."

Expected reactions & how to handle

REACTION · SCEPTICAL

"SEO is slow. We don't have 6 months."

Counter: Phase 1 goes live on Google Ads day one at €3K/mo. Paid captures intent while organic ranks. By Month 6 organic should be carrying 40%+. You are never paying full CAC for 6 months — the paid layer is a bridge, not the plan.

REACTION · PRICE

"€22K is too much for 5 pages."

Counter: It's not 5 pages — it's a hub, a rate calculator, CRM integration, bilingual copy, and the SEO/ads launch. Break-even at Month 3–4 on the conservative model. One extra India–UZ shipment/month pays for the whole build.

REACTION · SCOPE CUT

"Can we do 2 corridors instead of 5 to start?"

Push back: 5 is the viable floor. Less than 5 and Google treats us as a thin site, we don't accumulate topical authority, and the rate calculator can't cross-sell. Offer instead: 5 corridors, cheaper copy pass on 3 of them.

REACTION · BUYS IT

"OK. When can we start?"

Don't celebrate. Close: "Great. I need two things this week — (1) your top 5 corridor picks, (2) sign-off on the CRM integration scope. I'll have a contract on your desk by Friday and we start next Monday."

Slide-by-slide talking points

SLIDE 01 · COVER

Set the number before anything else.

"This is a €100–350K per year conversation, not a marketing conversation. Everything you'll see next is a defence of that number."
SLIDE 02 · WHAT YOUR TEAM TOLD US

Use his sales manager's words, by name.

"This isn't my idea. [Name of sales manager] told us — and administration confirmed — that the highest-margin freight we can win is long-haul into Central Asia. I'm showing you how to capture what he's already asking for."
  • Pause here. Let the CEO nod. If he doesn't, stop and ask what's different from what he's heard from the floor.
SLIDE 03 · MARKET GROWING

Read the growth numbers slowly.

"US to Uzbekistan: up 55%. India to Uzbekistan: up 37%. The Kazakhstan–India trade line crossed one billion. These are not marketing numbers. They're national statistics."
  • Be ready to challenge gently: If he says "those are small bases," acknowledge and pivot to the CKU railway as the structural shift — that's the long-term tailwind.
SLIDE 04 · SITE DOESN'T SAY IT

This is the uncomfortable slide. Stay factual.

"Your homepage presents all 9 services equally. A domestic delivery worth €400 and an India–Uzbekistan shipment worth €6,000 get the same pixel weight. That is a positioning problem and a conversion problem."
  • Do not call the site "bad." Call it "under-specialised." He owns it emotionally.
SLIDE 05 · WHAT BUYERS SEARCH

The "0 / 10,000" number is the moment.

"Ten thousand high-intent searches a month. Your site is visible on zero of them. Our competitors, mostly directories, are taking those clicks right now."
SLIDE 06 · WHO RANKS (SERP)

Use the word "capturable."

"This is the unusual part. Nobody owns these keywords. AZFreight is a directory. The global forwarders have generic pages. The Indian specialists don't have our EU banking or liability cover. The gap is yours if you move."
SLIDE 07 · FUNNEL MATH

Every assumption labeled CONSERVATIVE is deliberate.

"Every number is the middle or the lower half of the industry benchmark. 18% CTR in a range of 15–25%. 3% conversion in a range of 2–5%. If you think any number is wrong, tell me and I'll drag the slider live on the next slide."
  • Numbers to challenge gently if he pushes: €6,000 avg revenue and 18% margin. Those are the two he'll feel in his gut. Invite him to move them.
  • Keep the €103K headline. Don't let him debate it down to zero — invite him into the model instead.
SLIDE 08 · LIVE CALCULATOR

This is where you hand him the controller.

"Pick the sliders. Drop them to the worst number you think is defensible. Watch the number recompute. The model survives almost any hostile input and still clears €40K/yr."
  • Let him break it. Actually drag the sliders yourself if he doesn't.
  • If he tanks every slider to minimum, the result is still ~€12K/yr — and the build pays back.
SLIDE 09 · COST OF DOING NOTHING

This is the only slide where you raise the temperature.

"Logistics clients stick 3–5 years. Whoever books their first India–Uzbekistan shipment in 2026 keeps that relationship through 2029. A six-month delay isn't €50K; it's a full LTV chain permanently captured by someone else."
SLIDE 10 · ARCHITECTURE

Keep this short. It's a plan slide.

"One hub. Twenty children. Each child is a real page — not a template — with its own rate band, customs guidance, case study. Each is an SEO asset that keeps earning after launch."
SLIDE 11 · HERO REVEAL

Stop talking.

"This is what it looks like."
  • Then wait 5 seconds. Let him read. The mockup does the work here. Open hub.html and india-uzbekistan.html in browser tabs beforehand so you can go live.
  • Click through to the India–Uzbekistan child page. That's the proof the concept is buildable.
SLIDE 12 · ASK

Be specific. Ask for a yes, not a "think about it."

"I'm asking for €22K, an 8-week window, and your five corridor picks by Friday. Every week we delay is roughly €2,000 in margin. We should start next Monday."

Three things to push back on

  • Dropping below 5 corridors. Kills SEO authority and the calculator's cross-sell. Offer cheaper copy, not fewer corridors.
  • Skipping the Russian-language version at Phase 1 launch. Russian is the commercial language for target buyers in KZ and UZ. EN-only Phase 1 halves your TAM.
  • Cutting the rate calculator. It's the single highest-converting element on the page. Without it the hub is a brochure.

Three things you can give up if needed

  • Phase 3 (client portal). Nice-to-have. Can be deferred or descoped.
  • Kazakh and Uzbek language at Phase 2. Can slip to Q4. RU covers most intent short-term.
  • Live shipment counter on hero. Cosmetic. Replace with a static trust badge if operations pushes back.

What to bring to the room

  • The deck open on slide 1 — not slide 0. Don't start with a title screen.
  • The exec summary printed, one copy per attendee.
  • Two browser tabs pre-loaded: hub.html and india-uzbekistan.html.
  • A one-page list of proposed Phase 1 corridors in your order (so if he says "pick for me" you're not caught).
  • A blank notebook. Write down his five corridor choices on camera so it feels committed.
◆ CLOSE LINE · MEMORISE

"You have three paths. Approve Phase 1 and start compounding this week. Ask for edits and lose two weeks. Say no and give the next three years of India–Central Asia margin to someone else. Which one?"
